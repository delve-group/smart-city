## Task: Open Task — Smart City

Source: "Details - SMART CITY.pdf" from the HackYeah 2026 participant pack (received 2026-10-03).

A city is a system of connected services, infrastructure and everyday decisions. The task: pick a specific problem faced by a city or its residents, identify the users, and build a tool, app, system or prototype that helps people make better use of urban resources and services. Suggested areas:

- Transportation and journey planning.
- Accessibility of spaces and services for people with different needs.
- Management of energy, water, waste or shared infrastructure.
- Communication between residents and public institutions.
- Using urban data to support decisions.
- Responding to failures, disruptions and emergencies.

Scale may be a street, a neighbourhood, a service or a whole city. The submission should **show how the idea works in a concrete situation and who benefits**, and consider the accuracy and timeliness of information, ease of use, and whether it could be tested in real urban conditions. Prize pool: 8 000 PLN.

Team notes (translated): focus on mobility, energy, urban data, public services, quality of life or any related area — technology that helps cities work better and makes everyday city life easier.

## Use of existing resources and AI

- Existing repositories, materials and resources are allowed when properly cited.
- AI tools are allowed at every stage. **Significant use of AI tools and of external models, APIs, datasets and libraries must be disclosed in the submission.** The team must be able to explain and defend every technical decision, including AI-assisted code.
- Work done during HackYeah must be clearly distinguished from anything that existed before the event.
- Our disclosure list starts in [Architecture → Libraries](../architecture.md#libraries) (Next.js, Appica UI, MapLibre, OpenFreeMap/OpenStreetMap, Photon, Zod) plus AI coding assistance.

## Judging method

Judging has two stages:

1. **Review of submissions on the HackTribe platform.** Projects are scored by a committee appointed for the task, made up of at least three mentors. A project must score at least 50% of the points in this stage to be eligible for a prize.
2. **Finalist presentations to the jury.** Teams selected in the first stage advance. The jury scores live presentations. The first-stage committee and the second-stage jury may, but do not have to, overlap.

Jury members choose a chair among themselves. In case of a tie in either stage, the chair has the casting vote. Decisions of the jury and its members are final and not subject to appeal.

## Criteria and weights

| Criterion (original name) | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Idea & Innovation | 30% |
| Relation to Category | 20% |
| Practical Applicability / Usability | 20% |
| Design | 20% |
| Completeness & Implementation Value | 10% |

The rules give criterion names and weights but publish no detailed scale or sub-criteria. Working interpretations of these names should not be treated as official scoring rules.

## Submission requirements relevant to judging

- Submissions are uploaded to the Challenge Rocket platform (the task details name it; the general terms mention HackTribe) in Polish or English.
- A submission should include the project title, team name, a list of 1–6 team members and a project description.
- A PDF presentation of up to 10 slides is required by the task details (the general terms list it as optional). It can include screenshots, a code repository, demo links and graphics; the detailed task description may list additional materials.
- Work may start no earlier than 23:00 on 3 October, and the submission must be made by 23:00 on 4 October at the latest.
- Changes made after the deadline are not considered by the jury.
